Remote learning does more harm than good. The strongest evidence concerns inequality: students from poorer households are far less likely to have a quiet study space or a reliable device. Consequently, they fall further behind, and the gap widens over time.

A second concern is motivation. Without the structure of a school day, many students struggle to organise their work. For instance, a national survey reported that secondary pupils submitted fewer assignments during remote periods. Nevertheless, some students thrive online because they can work at their own pace.

Opponents argue that remote learning is flexible and convenient. While this is true for a minority, the overall pattern of falling attainment and increased isolation suggests that it does more harm than good for most secondary students.

In conclusion, although remote learning has advantages, its costs to equity, motivation and wellbeing outweigh them.
