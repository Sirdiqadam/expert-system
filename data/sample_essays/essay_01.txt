School uniforms should be abolished. They place an unfair financial burden on families, they suppress students' individuality, and they do little to improve behaviour. For these reasons, schools should abandon mandatory uniforms and allow students to dress appropriately in their own clothes.

The most obvious objection to uniforms is their cost. Families with several children must buy a complete set of uniforms, often at specialist shops, and replace them as the children grow. For example, a single blazer can cost more than seventy dollars, and many parents struggle to afford several of them each year.

Furthermore, uniforms limit self-expression. Teenagers are developing their identities and need some freedom in how they present themselves. Some may argue that uniforms reduce inequality, yet a visible gap between expensive new uniforms and hand-me-downs persists.

In conclusion, uniforms cost too much and restrict students. Schools should therefore replace them with a reasonable dress code.
