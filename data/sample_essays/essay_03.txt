Remote learning does more harm than good for secondary students. While it offers flexibility, it widens inequality, harms learning outcomes, and damages social development.

Access is the clearest problem. Many students lack reliable internet or a quiet place to study. According to recent surveys, around one in five students missed lessons because of poor connectivity. As a result, those students fell further behind their peers.

Learning outcomes also suffer. Without a teacher nearby, students receive less immediate feedback and are more likely to procrastinate. For example, a study of secondary pupils found that online learners completed fewer assignments than those in classrooms.

Finally, remote learning isolates students. School is where teenagers build friendships and social skills. Some may argue that video calls replace this contact, but they cannot reproduce everyday interaction.

Therefore, although flexibility is useful, the costs to equality, outcomes and wellbeing mean remote learning should not replace classroom teaching.
